BIOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION
-------------------------
sample_01:
SBS15 is associated with defective DNA mismatch repair.
SBS1 is associated with spontaneous deamination of 5-methylcytosine.
The dominant SBS15 exposure suggests a possible mismatch repair-related process.

sample_02:
SBS7a, SBS7b, and SBS7d are associated with ultraviolet (UV) damage.
The profile is consistent with UV-associated mutagenesis.

sample_03:
SBS7a and SBS7b are dominant and indicate UV-associated mutagenesis.
SBS31 is additionally assigned by the fitting procedure.

sample_04:
SBS4 is associated with tobacco smoking.
SBS42 and SBS24 also appear in the fitted exposure profile.
Interpretation of minor signatures requires caution.

sample_05:
SBS7b and SBS7a dominate the reconstructed mutational profile.
The result suggests UV-associated mutagenesis.

sample_06:
SBS13 and SBS2 are associated with APOBEC cytidine deaminase activity.
The result is consistent with an APOBEC-associated mutational process.

sample_07:
SBS13 and SBS2 suggest APOBEC activity.
SBS3 is associated with homologous recombination repair deficiency,
but this assignment alone is insufficient to establish HR deficiency.

sample_08:
SBS2 and SBS13 suggest APOBEC-associated mutagenesis.
The relatively small number of mutations limits interpretation.

sample_09:
SBS29 is associated with chewing tobacco exposure in COSMIC.
SBS4 is associated with tobacco smoking.
However, this sample has the lowest cosine similarity (0.7674),
so the biological interpretation is particularly uncertain.

QUALITY CONTROL AND LIMITATIONS
-------------------------------
- All 1359 SNVs were assigned to SBS96 channels.
- Reference bases were validated against GRCh37/hg19.
- All nine samples had zero invalid trinucleotide contexts.
- The fitting procedure used 60 COSMIC v3.2 SBS signatures.
- The reconstructed mutation totals differ somewhat from observed totals.
- High cosine similarity indicates a good reconstruction of the observed
  SBS96 profile, not definitive identification of causal processes.
- Fitting many reference signatures to samples with few mutations can
  produce unstable or overfitted signature assignments.
- In particular, sample_06, sample_07, sample_08, and sample_09
  contain relatively few SNVs.
- Signature assignments should be treated as hypotheses rather
  than definitive biological diagnoses.

CONCLUSION
----------
The nine samples show distinct fitted mutational signature patterns:

- Samples 02, 03, and 05: predominantly UV-associated SBS7 signatures.
- Samples 06, 07, and 08: APOBEC-associated SBS2/SBS13 signatures.
- Sample 01: predominantly mismatch repair-associated SBS15.
- Samples 04 and 09: fitted tobacco-associated signatures.

These findings require cautious interpretation because signature fitting
is sensitive to mutation burden, signature overlap, and model complexity.
